Dear Editors of Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction,

Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled

  "Choosing the Bottleneck Dimension of Autoencoders and Variational
   Autoencoders from the Intrinsic Dimension of the Data: An ID-Guided
   Protocol with Active-Unit Verification"

by Chika Obata, Mizuki Dai, and Kenya Jin'no, which we would like to
submit for consideration as an Article in Machine Learning and Knowledge
Extraction (MAKE).

The bottleneck dimension of autoencoders (AEs) and variational
autoencoders (VAEs) is their most fundamental design parameter, yet it is
usually chosen by convention or trial and error. Our manuscript proposes a
conditional, diagnostic ID-guided protocol that closes the loop of
estimation, setting, and verification: (i) a search window for the
bottleneck dimension m is derived from an estimate of the intrinsic
dimension of the input data (AE: m in [d_ID, 2 d_ID]; VAE: m >= 2 d_ID);
(ii) after training, the active units (AU) of the VAE diagnose whether the
surplus dimensions were actually truncated. The protocol is grounded in a
topological lower bound for self-intersection-free reconstruction and in a
water-filling activation condition proven for a linear-Gaussian surrogate.
We validate the three protocol steps on synthetic manifolds with known
ground truth and on fully connected VAEs on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST with
multi-seed decision rules, and we explicitly quantify the limits of
applicability on convolutional VAEs and natural images. On the MNIST grid
used in the paper, the ID-guided window reduces the training runs of a
full grid search by roughly 60-80% while attaining the grid-best
downstream linear-probe accuracy inside the window.

We believe this work fits the scope of MAKE: it extracts actionable
knowledge (a principled model-selection rule) from the geometry of data,
combines theory with reproducible multi-seed experiments, and candidly
delineates the conditions under which the extracted rule is valid.
