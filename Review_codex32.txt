Review of main_paper_MAKE32.pdf
Target journal: MDPI Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction (MAKE)
Reviewer: Codex
Date: 2026-07-07

Recommendation: Major Revision

Summary
-------
This manuscript proposes a multi-indicator diagnostic framework for interpreting
effective latent dimensionality in AEs and VAEs. The framework combines active
units (AU), intrinsic-dimension estimates by TwoNN/MLE, reconstruction-error
elbows, and PCA baselines. A linear-Gaussian rate-distortion surrogate is used
to derive an AU activation threshold, lambda_j > beta tau^2, and a set of
experiments on synthetic manifolds, MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, Conv-VAEs, CIFAR-10,
and SVHN is used to characterize where the indicators are stable or unstable.

The manuscript has a useful and timely goal: it tries to move away from a
single-number estimate of latent dimensionality and instead clarifies the
diagnostic role and reliability of multiple indicators. The paper is also
commendably cautious in several places, especially in treating m_knee as an
exploratory indicator rather than a unique optimum. However, the current draft
is not yet ready for publication. The main issues are the strength of the
claims relative to the evidence, the circularity and bias of the reference-AE
TwoNN estimate, the fragility of m_knee, and the need for clearer experimental
protocols and reproducibility details.

Major Comments
--------------
1. The central quantity "effective latent dimensionality" still needs a sharper
operational definition.

The paper repeatedly states that the goal is diagnostic interpretation rather
than a unique estimate. This is a reasonable position, but the manuscript still
uses several quantities as if they measure the same target: AU(m), TwoNN ID of a
reference AE, MLE ID, MSE elbow, PCA variance dimension, m_knee, AU saturation,
and sometimes topological embedding dimension. The reader needs a more precise
definition of what is being estimated in each setting.

Suggested revision:
- Add a short subsection that explicitly distinguishes:
  (i) data-manifold intrinsic dimension,
  (ii) latent representation intrinsic dimension,
  (iii) number of KL-active VAE coordinates,
  (iv) bottleneck dimension needed for reconstruction,
  (v) diagnostic design dimension.
- State which claims concern each quantity. For example, AU is not an intrinsic
  dimension estimator; it is a beta-, architecture-, threshold-, and training-
  dependent measure of coordinate usage.

2. The dense-grid m_knee result is promising but not fully independent.

The manuscript reports sparse-grid m_knee values of 43 +/- 15 or 53 +/- 15 on
MNIST, but dense-grid m_knee = 10.3 +/- 0.9 after searching m = 8--16 densely,
near the TwoNN estimate of about 10. This supports the practical value of
dense search, but it also introduces a dependence on the prior TwoNN estimate.
Because the candidate region was chosen using the same framework, the agreement
between dense-grid m_knee and TwoNN is not a fully independent validation.

Suggested revision:
- Reframe the result as "TwoNN-guided local refinement of m_knee" rather than
  as independent confirmation.
- Add an experiment or analysis using a pre-specified dense grid over a broader
  range, or use nested validation: estimate the dense-search region on one
  subset/seed set and evaluate m_knee on disjoint seeds or data.
- Report the exact per-seed AU curves for all dense-grid runs, not only
  representative values for seed 42.

3. The instability of m_knee should be treated as a first-class limitation, not
only as something solved by grid density.

The paper correctly shows that m_knee can vary widely on sparse grids and that
the AU curve can be non-monotone. However, the final narrative sometimes reads
as if dense grids and multi-seed runs are sufficient to stabilize m_knee in
general. The evidence supports this strongly only for MNIST FC-VAE under the
specific beta=4, architecture, and dense-grid design. Fashion-MNIST is
single-seed in the main experiment, Conv-VAE requires much larger m or beta,
and natural images do not satisfy the proposed stability criteria.

Suggested revision:
- In the abstract and conclusion, make the scope of the stabilized m_knee
  result explicit: MNIST FC-VAE, beta=4, dense grid near the estimated ID,
  three seeds.
- Avoid wording such as "recommended for use" unless the required conditions
  are stated immediately.
- Consider using "m_knee is a TwoNN-guided exploratory change point" as the
  default description.

4. The reference-AE TwoNN estimate is stable, but its accuracy remains uncertain.

The manuscript acknowledges isometry bias and possible circularity, which is
important. However, the paper still uses the reference-AE TwoNN estimate as a
major anchor for the entire diagnostic framework. The Exp-Real-Geom results
show that increasing m/d stabilizes TwoNN, but they do not prove that the
stable value is unbiased with respect to the true data manifold. A stable
biased estimator can still guide a diagnostic pipeline, but the claims should
be more cautious.

Suggested revision:
- State more prominently that TwoNN provides a stable latent-space reference,
  not necessarily the true intrinsic dimension of the input distribution.
- For MNIST, compare with raw-pixel TwoNN/MLE, PCA-space TwoNN, and possibly
  other ID estimators such as DANCo or ESS, at least in a supplementary table.
- Report confidence intervals or bootstrap variability for TwoNN/MLE estimates,
  not only seed standard deviations.

5. The theoretical contribution should be positioned more conservatively.

The linear-Gaussian rate-distortion surrogate and the threshold
lambda_j > beta tau^2 are useful for interpretation, but the result is closely
related to known PPCA, rate-distortion, water-filling, and linear-VAE analyses.
The paper states that the theorem quantifies prior qualitative arguments. This
is acceptable, but the novelty should not be overstated.

Suggested revision:
- Emphasize that the theorem is an interpretive surrogate and not a theorem
  about trained nonlinear VAEs.
- Clarify the relation to PPCA and linear VAE results in more detail.
- In the theorem statement, define precisely whether lambda_j is a covariance
  eigenvalue in input space, PCA space, or an effective local spectrum.
- Move some derivation details to the appendix and keep the main text focused
  on assumptions, implications, and limitations.

6. Several experimental claims require stronger reproducibility support.

The paper contains many experiments, but the level of replication is uneven.
Some key claims are based on three seeds, while others are explicitly
single-seed or preliminary. Three seeds are acceptable for an exploratory
study, but the paper should avoid broad claims based on them.

Suggested revision:
- Add a compact experiment table with, for each experiment: dataset, model,
  epochs, beta schedule, m grid, number of seeds, sample size, and whether the
  result is main/supplementary/preliminary.
- Mark all single-seed results in tables and captions, not only in prose.
- For central claims, include per-seed values and standard deviations for both
  AU and TwoNN/MLE.
- Provide code, configs, and raw logs as supplementary material at submission,
  not only after acceptance if possible.

7. The natural-image section should be shortened and reframed.

The CIFAR-10/SVHN experiments are useful because they identify boundary
conditions, but the current draft devotes substantial space to conditions under
which the framework does not achieve stable identification. This makes the
paper long and can obscure the main MNIST/synthetic contribution.

Suggested revision:
- Move some natural-image sweeps to the appendix.
- In the main text, keep only the main lesson: TwoNN can be numerically stable
  near the known CIFAR-10 ID scale, while AU/m_knee is not stably identified
  without high beta and restricted decoder capacity.
- Avoid implying that the natural-image experiments validate the full
  framework. They mainly define its current boundary.

8. The proposed stable-identification criteria are useful but appear ad hoc.

The five criteria for stable identification are sensible engineering criteria,
but the thresholds are not theoretically justified and are introduced late in
the paper. This is acceptable if framed as operational criteria, but the
manuscript should avoid treating them as general validity conditions.

Suggested revision:
- Introduce the criteria earlier, before the natural-image evaluation.
- State that the thresholds are author-defined operational thresholds.
- Explain why 10%, 20%, 5%, and 3*d_ID were selected, or provide sensitivity
  analysis showing that conclusions do not depend strongly on these choices.

Minor Comments
--------------
1. The manuscript is too long and repetitive for a standard article. Many
sentences repeatedly state that m_knee is exploratory and TwoNN is auxiliary.
This message is important, but it can be consolidated.

2. Some tables are dense and difficult to read. Consider moving large
multi-condition tables to the appendix and using summary plots in the main
text.

3. Use consistent terminology for "active units", "AU values", "AU saturation",
"AUsat", "effective dimensionality", and "diagnostic candidate dimension".

4. The abstract is long and contains many detailed numbers. It should be
shortened and focused on the problem, method, key result, and limitation.

5. The Fashion-MNIST results may confuse readers because the main experiment
and tau-sensitivity appendix give different absolute values under different
setups. The current caveat helps, but the paper should reduce direct
cross-comparison or place the appendix result clearly as a separate protocol.

6. The statement that Fashion-MNIST may have "fewer local degrees of freedom
than handwritten digits" should be softened unless supported by more rigorous
analysis.

7. The author-contribution statement lists K.J. as writing the original draft
and formal analysis. Ensure that all author roles comply with MDPI authorship
and contribution expectations.

8. Check that all figures referenced through IfFileExists are actually included
in the submission package. Missing figures would severely weaken readability.

9. The data-availability statement says code and logs will be deposited upon
acceptance. For a computational paper, providing them for review is strongly
recommended.

10. The paper should include hardware details and runtime estimates for the
main experiments, especially because the framework requires many model
trainings across m grids and seeds.

Overall Assessment
------------------
The manuscript has a worthwhile idea and a substantial amount of experimental
work. Its strongest contribution is not a new universal estimator of latent
dimension, but a careful diagnostic separation of AU, TwoNN/MLE, reconstruction
error, PCA, and m_knee, together with empirical evidence that AU and TwoNN can
be stable while m_knee is highly protocol-dependent.

For publication in MAKE, I recommend major revision. The authors should narrow
the claims, define the target quantities more sharply, present the dense-grid
m_knee result as TwoNN-guided rather than independent, strengthen
reproducibility reporting, and shorten or reorganize the natural-image
boundary experiments. With these changes, the paper could become a useful
methodological contribution for researchers diagnosing latent representations
in AEs and VAEs.
