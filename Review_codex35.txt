Review of main_paper_MAKE35.pdf
Target journal: MDPI Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction (MAKE)
Reviewer: Codex
Date: 2026-07-08

Overall recommendation: Major revision

Summary
-------
This manuscript proposes an ID-guided protocol for selecting the bottleneck
dimension of AEs/VAEs: estimate intrinsic dimension (ID), set the bottleneck
dimension with a topological margin, and verify the trained VAE using active
units (AU). The paper combines a topological lower-bound argument, a
linear-Gaussian water-filling surrogate for AU activation, and empirical
validation on synthetic manifolds, MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, Conv-VAEs, and natural
images.

The topic is relevant to MAKE and the manuscript is substantially improved by
its explicit protocol, multi-seed reporting in the main experiments, and honest
discussion of applicability limits. However, the current version still relies
on several qualitative bridges between estimated ID, bottleneck selection, and
AU slowdown. The paper is promising, but the central claims should be tightened
and several experimental and presentation issues should be addressed before
submission.

Major comments
--------------
1. The status of the central claim should be weakened or formalized more
   carefully.

   The manuscript asks whether the bottleneck dimension can be chosen from the
   intrinsic dimension and answers affirmatively. Yet the actual validated
   protocol is more conditional: it gives a useful reference range on MNIST and
   synthetic manifolds, while Conv-VAEs and natural images require substantially
   stronger beta, decoder-capacity control, or remain future work. The abstract
   and conclusion should avoid sounding like a general solution. A more accurate
   framing is: "ID estimates can guide a candidate range, and AU can diagnose
   whether the VAE actually truncates surplus dimensions under suitable beta and
   architecture."

2. AU slowdown is used as a verification signal, but the decision rule remains
   under-specified.

   The paper wisely removes automatic knee detection as a named output, because
   the detected knee is grid-dependent. However, Step 3 then relies on a
   qualitative judgment: "saturates/clearly slows down near d_ID." For a protocol
   paper, readers need a reproducible rule for deciding pass/fail, even if it is
   deliberately conservative. For example, the authors could report AU/m,
   finite-difference slopes, and a predefined tolerance window such as
   AU-growth slope below a stated threshold over a fixed log-spaced grid. If the
   authors do not want an algorithmic rule, the protocol should be presented as
   a diagnostic workflow rather than a selection algorithm.

3. The empirical validation of the full protocol is narrower than the
   contribution statement suggests.

   The full three-step protocol with multi-seed AU verification is convincingly
   demonstrated mainly for synthetic manifolds and MNIST FC-VAEs. Fashion-MNIST
   has multi-seed Step-1 evidence but only single-seed VAE-side verification;
   high-beta Conv-VAE recovery is single-seed exploratory; natural-image Step 3
   is not achieved in the main setting. These limitations are stated in the
   paper, but the contributions and abstract should mirror them more directly.
   Otherwise reviewers may view the work as overclaiming relative to the
   evidence.

4. The link between topological embedding dimension and VAE AU needs sharper
   interpretation.

   Proposition 1 concerns exact continuous reconstruction without
   self-intersection and gives a necessary condition involving d_emb. The VAE AU
   results on the torus suggest AUsat = d_emb, but the manuscript also notes that
   this is conditional and not theoretically necessary. The main text should be
   careful not to imply that AU estimates d_emb. A useful addition would be a
   short paragraph explicitly distinguishing:
   - d_ID estimated by TwoNN/MLE,
   - d_emb required for exact embedding,
   - AU as a beta- and architecture-dependent usage count.
   This distinction is present in parts of the text, but it should be reiterated
   where the synthetic results are interpreted.

5. The TwoNN-through-reference-AE procedure needs stronger validation against
   estimator bias.

   The manuscript acknowledges that stable estimates are not necessarily
   unbiased. This is important, because the protocol depends on d_hat_ID as the
   center of the candidate range. The paper would be strengthened by adding
   bootstrap confidence intervals, estimator subsampling sensitivity, or at least
   a table comparing direct input-space estimates, reference-AE estimates, MLE,
   and one additional estimator where feasible. DANCo is mentioned as future
   work, but even a limited cross-check would reduce the risk that the protocol
   is tuned to TwoNN behavior.

6. The baseline comparison should be expanded.

   The paper contrasts the protocol with reconstruction-error sweeps and PCA,
   but it does not quantify whether the proposed range reduces search cost or
   preserves downstream utility. For MAKE readers, it would help to include a
   compact comparison such as:
   - full m-grid search vs. ID-guided candidate range,
   - reconstruction MSE / ELBO at selected m,
   - simple downstream classification accuracy from latent representations,
   - number of training runs saved.
   This would clarify the practical value beyond interpretability.

7. The water-filling result is useful but should be explicitly labeled as a
   rationale, not an empirical predictor.

   Proposition 2 is correctly limited to a linear-Gaussian approximation, and
   the manuscript states that nonlinear use is interpretive. Still, phrases such
   as "guarantees" or "self-repairs" may be too strong when discussing nonlinear
   VAEs. Replace stronger wording with "predicts in the surrogate model" or
   "motivates the diagnostic expectation." The heuristic tau_eff discussion
   should also remain clearly separated from the proposition.

8. The Conv-VAE and natural-image results are valuable but currently read as a
   mixture of boundary analysis and incomplete application.

   This section is one of the manuscript's strengths because it documents
   failure modes. To avoid confusing the reader, add a short "verdict" after
   each subsection:
   - Step 1 usable / not usable under stated m_ref range,
   - Step 2 candidate range obtained / not obtained,
   - Step 3 AU verification passed / failed / unresolved.
   This would make the boundary analysis fit the protocol structure more
   cleanly.

Minor comments and presentation issues
--------------------------------------
1. The abstract is very dense. Consider shortening the theoretical details and
   moving some caveats into the final sentence. The current abstract may be hard
   for non-specialist MAKE readers to parse.

2. The term "correct dimension" should be avoided for real data. Use "reference
   estimate," "consistent dimension range," or "ground-truth dimension" only for
   synthetic manifolds.

3. The phrase "m comfortably above d_hat_ID" should be operationalized. The
   protocol later says AE: [d_hat_ID, 2d_hat_ID], VAE: m >= 2d_hat_ID. Use this
   more concrete wording consistently.

4. Table 6 mixes a single-seed sweep with a 3-seed value in the caption. This is
   acceptable if unavoidable, but the table should visually separate single-seed
   and multi-seed entries to prevent misreading.

5. In Section 5.3.3, the statement that AE-side MSE slows at m approximately
   16-20 appears to reference Table 6, but Table 6 is a reference-AE sweep rather
   than a direct AE model-selection table. Consider adding the relevant AE MSE
   table or clarifying the connection.

6. Figure 3 reports per-seed mknee values despite the paper saying knee
   detection is not a named output. This is fine as exploratory context, but the
   figure label may unintentionally re-elevate mknee. Consider moving mknee
   labels to Appendix B or labeling them explicitly as exploratory.

7. The manuscript mentions supplementary material for review and future
   GitHub/Zenodo deposition. For submission, include a clear supplementary-file
   inventory and ensure that scripts reproduce the exact tables.

8. The related-work section is concise but could mention model-selection and
   latent-dimensionality selection literature beyond ID estimation, including
   Bayesian nonparametric latent variable models or automatic relevance
   determination, if relevant.

9. Check MDPI formatting requirements before submission: line numbers, reference
   style, data-availability wording, and whether "Submitted to Mach. Learn.
   Knowl. Extr." and placeholder DOI are acceptable in the generated template.

10. Some figures are visually dense in the PDF text extraction, especially
    Figures 2-5. Before submission, verify that axis labels, legends, and
    annotations remain readable at journal column width.

Strengths
---------
1. The manuscript addresses a practical and under-specified design problem:
   choosing the bottleneck dimension of AEs/VAEs.

2. The proposed three-step protocol is easy to understand and potentially useful
   for practitioners.

3. The distinction among d_ID, d_emb, d_hat_ID, AU, and AUsat is conceptually
   valuable and helps avoid a common conflation between intrinsic dimension and
   latent usage.

4. The paper is unusually explicit about limitations, including grid sensitivity
   of knee detection, estimator stability versus validity, and Conv-VAE beta
   dependence.

5. The synthetic-manifold experiments are well chosen for illustrating why a
   topological margin may be needed.

Recommended revision priorities
-------------------------------
1. Reframe the main claim as a conditional, diagnostic protocol rather than a
   generally solved selection rule.

2. Add a reproducible AU-verification criterion or explicitly state that Step 3
   is a qualitative diagnostic with required plots/statistics.

3. Strengthen or better qualify the empirical evidence for Fashion-MNIST,
   Conv-VAEs, and natural images.

4. Add practical baseline comparisons showing search-cost reduction and
   retained reconstruction/downstream performance.

5. Clarify all single-seed versus multi-seed evidence in captions and claims.

Conclusion
----------
The paper is suitable in topic and style for MAKE, and it contains a useful
protocol with honest boundary analysis. I would not recommend submission in its
current form without revision, mainly because the central claims are broader
than the fully validated evidence and because the AU-verification rule is not
yet sufficiently operational. With the above revisions, the manuscript could
become a strong applied-methods contribution.
