Review of main_paper_MAKE36.pdf
Target journal: MDPI Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction (MAKE)
Reviewer: Codex
Date: 2026-07-08

Overall recommendation: Minor revision

Summary
-------
This manuscript proposes a diagnostic protocol for choosing the bottleneck
dimension of AEs and VAEs from an intrinsic-dimension estimate: estimate
d_hat_ID, choose an AE/VAE bottleneck range with a topological margin, and
verify VAE usage post hoc using active units (AU). The revision substantially
improves the previous version. It now states the claim as conditional rather
than universal, adds a reproducible V1/V2 AU decision rule, reports
subsampling confidence intervals and cross-estimator checks for Step 1,
quantifies search-cost savings with a linear-probe baseline, and labels the
Conv-VAE, Fashion-MNIST, and natural-image results more honestly as boundary
or exploratory evidence.

The paper is now close to publishable as an applied-methods contribution, but
several issues should still be fixed before acceptance. The most important
remaining weaknesses are an inconsistency in the synthetic torus AU evidence,
the still ad hoc nature of the V1/V2 thresholds and verification-point choice,
some over-interpretation of the AE candidate range and water-filling
surrogate, and the limited validation beyond MNIST fully connected models.

Major comments
--------------
1. The torus AU result is internally inconsistent and affects the claim that
   the synthetic full protocol cleanly passes.

   Table 4 reports Torus VAE AU = 2.0 at m = 4, but the text states that the
   Torus AU saturates at AUsat = 3 = d_emb when m is set above d_emb. Since
   m = 4 is already above d_emb = 3 and also satisfies the protocol condition
   m_ver >= 2 d_hat_ID with d_hat_ID approximately 2, this is not a minor
   cosmetic issue. The subsequent verdict instead evaluates the Torus at
   m_ver = 6 = 3 d_hat_ID, where AU = 3 and V1/V2 pass.

   The authors should clarify whether m = 4 is a genuine under-activation
   case, an optimization artifact, or a table/reporting error. If it is
   genuine, the text should not claim sharp saturation at d_emb for all
   m > d_emb, and the protocol should specify how m_ver is chosen. As written,
   V1/V2 would also pass at m = 4 (AU/m = 0.5 and 2 <= AU <= 6), even though
   the interpretation that AU reports d_emb would fail. This reveals a
   limitation of the rule that should be explicitly discussed.

2. The V1/V2 decision rule is useful, but its operating characteristics are
   still insufficiently justified.

   The addition of V1 (AU/m <= 0.9) and V2 (d_hat_ID <= AU <= 3 d_hat_ID) is a
   real improvement over a qualitative knee detector. However, the thresholds
   remain working defaults calibrated on this manuscript cases rather than
   a validated rule. V2 is especially permissive: AU values up to three times
   d_hat_ID are accepted, which can mask substantial over-use of latent
   dimensions. Conversely, the lower bound d_hat_ID <= AU can conflict with
   the paper caution that AU is not an ID estimator and is beta- and
   architecture-dependent.

   Please add a compact threshold-sensitivity table for the V1/V2 verdicts, or
   state more explicitly that V1/V2 is a conservative reporting convention, not
   a statistically validated test. It would also be better to evaluate V1/V2
   per seed, or report confidence intervals for the verdict, rather than only
   applying the rule to the multi-seed mean AU.

3. The AE range [d_hat_ID, 2 d_hat_ID] is plausible but still over-presented
   as a general candidate range.

   The topological lower-bound argument only says that m must be at least
   d_emb for exact self-intersection-free reconstruction. It does not imply
   that 2 d_hat_ID is sufficient for real data, noisy data, unions of class
   manifolds, or manifolds with more complex topology and sampling geometry.
   The torus motivates a margin, but it does not validate the specific factor
   of 2 beyond the tested examples. The paper does acknowledge this in places,
   but the abstract, Figure 1, and conclusion still read as if the range has a
   stronger general status than the evidence supports.

   I recommend calling [d_hat_ID, 2 d_hat_ID] a default search window rather
   than a principled candidate range, and stating that larger margins may be
   required when topology, noise, class mixtures, or reconstruction fidelity
   demand it.

4. The water-filling proposition remains a rationale, not a predictor of
   empirical AU, and a few links should be tightened.

   Proposition 2 is correctly restricted to a linear-Gaussian
   rate-distortion approximation, and the revision now flags the
   aggregated-posterior mismatch. Still, the empirical AU definition is a
   threshold on posterior-mean variance, whereas the proposition concerns
   activated eigendirections/rate allocation. The connection between these two
   notions is plausible but not exact. The effective-noise explanation for
   Conv-VAEs is also post hoc and architecture-dependent.

   The manuscript should avoid phrases implying that the surrogate explains
   or predicts the nonlinear AU counts quantitatively. A short bridge
   paragraph explaining when positive rate in the surrogate should correspond
   to nonzero posterior-mean variance in AU would make the theory section more
   defensible.

5. Step-1 robustness is improved, but estimator validity remains only partly
   established.

   The new subsampling intervals and DANCo/ESS checks are valuable. However,
   they quantify sampling variability on single trained reference-AE latents,
   not the full uncertainty induced by reference-model training, architecture,
   preprocessing, and estimator bias. Raw-space MNIST estimates are also not
   entirely dismissed by the data: raw TwoNN = 12.63 and raw MLE = 14.46 are
   not wildly different from the latent estimates, while ESS is higher and
   DANCo degenerates. The discussion should therefore avoid implying that the
   reference-AE route is uniquely validated; it is a pragmatic route that
   stabilizes the selected estimators under the authors conditions.

   The statement that PCA grossly overestimates the nonlinear manifold
   structure should also be softened. PCA cumulative-variance dimensions are
   not intrinsic-dimension estimates, so the comparison is illustrative rather
   than a direct baseline.

6. The empirical scope is now clearly stated, but it remains narrow for the
   stronger practical claims.

   The full multi-seed protocol is convincingly demonstrated mainly on
   synthetic manifolds and MNIST FC-VAEs. Fashion-MNIST Step 3 remains
   single-seed; high-beta Conv-VAE recovery is single-seed; natural-image Step
   3 is unresolved; and the downstream validation is a linear probe on MNIST
   with the AE side single-seed. These limitations are honestly reported, but
   they should continue to constrain the abstract and conclusion. In
   particular, the search-cost reduction should be described as demonstrated
   on the MNIST grid used in this paper, not as a general 60--80% reduction.

7. The topological lower-bound proposition should include its mathematical
   assumptions more explicitly.

   The proof relies on exact zero reconstruction, continuity, and the
   existence of a continuous inverse on the embedded image. This is safest
   under compactness or an explicit topological-embedding assumption. For real
   neural AEs with finite error, noise, and non-injective behavior on small
   sets, the proposition is only an idealized necessary condition. The paper
   mostly says this, but the proposition/proof should make the compactness or
   embedding condition explicit to avoid overclaiming.

Minor comments and presentation issues
--------------------------------------
1. The abstract is still dense. It now contains the main caveats, which is
   good, but the number of numerical claims and qualifications makes it hard
   to parse. Consider shortening the Conv-VAE/natural-image caveats and moving
   some detail to the introduction.

2. In Table 4, explicitly mark the Torus m = 4 AU = 2 row as not saturated
   if the value is retained. This would prevent readers from missing the
   mismatch with the surrounding text.

3. In the protocol section, define whether m_ver should be exactly
   ceil(2 d_hat_ID), any point above that value, or a pre-specified larger
   verification point. The current examples use different choices.

4. The phrase all three steps pass should be used carefully for settings
   where Step 1 and Step 3 are evaluated on different representations or
   where the verification point was selected after observing the AU curve.

5. Table 7 search-cost accounting counts Step-1 runs as 3 single-seed
   runs but counts the full grid as 11 x 3 seeds. This is reasonable as an
   illustrative comparison, but a fair operational protocol might also repeat
   Step 1 across seeds. State this assumption explicitly.

6. The raw-input DANCo result 784 (degenerate) needs a short explanation in
   the caption or text. Otherwise readers may wonder whether this is a
   software failure, a known estimator limitation, or a meaningful estimate.

7. The term trustworthy is used for the natural-image Step-1 estimate near
   m_ref approximately 2 d_hat_ID. Consider using consistent with prior
   estimates instead, since true ID is unknown and SVHN shows substantial
   estimator disagreement.

8. The discussion of condition number kappa should be cautious. A very large
   decoder-Jacobian condition number is a local geometric diagnostic, but it
   does not by itself fully characterize the neighbor-rank distortions that
   drive TwoNN.

9. Make sure the supplementary material is complete and easy to audit. The
   manuscript promises source code, configuration files, random seeds,
   trained-model logs, and numerical results for all tables. This is important
   because several claims depend on seed/grid choices.

10. MDPI formatting readiness should be checked before submission: empty
    received/revised/accepted dates, placeholder DOI, line numbering, ORCID
    placeholders, reference formatting, and final repository/DOI information
    should all be resolved according to the journal template.

Strengths
---------
1. The paper addresses a real practical gap: bottleneck dimensions in
   AEs/VAEs are often chosen by convention or exhaustive sweeps.

2. The revised manuscript is much more honest and precise about scope than the
   previous version. It no longer presents the method as a general solution.

3. The distinction among d_ID, d_emb, d_hat_ID, AU, AUsat, and dstar(beta) is
   conceptually valuable and helps avoid common misinterpretations.

4. The V1/V2 rule, although still heuristic, is a useful step toward
   reproducible AU-based reporting.

5. The added subsampling intervals, DANCo/ESS cross-checks, and search-cost
   comparison materially improve the empirical section.

6. The boundary analysis for Conv-VAEs and natural images is a strength. It
   shows where the protocol fails or becomes unresolved, rather than hiding
   those cases.

7. The appendix on change-point sensitivity is helpful and supports the
   authors decision not to make automatic knee detection a named protocol
   output.

Recommended revision priorities
-------------------------------
1. Fix or explicitly explain the Torus AU inconsistency at m = 4, and align
   the synthetic conclusions with the corrected interpretation.

2. Specify the verification-point selection rule for V1/V2 and report
   threshold/per-seed robustness of the verdicts.

3. Soften the generality of the AE [d_hat_ID, 2 d_hat_ID] range and the
   claimed search-cost savings.

4. Tighten the bridge between the water-filling surrogate and empirical AU,
   keeping nonlinear claims qualitative.

5. Clarify the remaining uncertainty in Step-1 estimator validity and avoid
   treating PCA variance dimensions as direct ID baselines.

6. Complete MDPI formatting and supplementary-material readiness checks.

Conclusion
----------
This revision resolves many of the major concerns from the previous round and
is now close to suitable for MAKE. The manuscript offers a useful conditional
workflow, meaningful diagnostics, and unusually clear failure-mode analysis.
Before acceptance, the authors should fix the torus/protocol inconsistency,
make the V1/V2 rule and verification-point choice more precise, and soften the
remaining claims that exceed the demonstrated evidence. These changes appear
substantial in importance but limited in scope, so I recommend minor revision.
